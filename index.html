Selected Research & Manuscripts

Dynamic Malicious Skills in Agentic AI

Tianhao Chen*, Zhengyuan Jiang*, Yuepeng Hu, Yebei Gou, and Neil Zhenqiang Gong

arXiv:2606.16287, 2026 · Under review, ACL Rolling Review (August 2026 cycle)

Studies documentation-borne threats that cause malicious behavior to emerge in otherwise benign skills at runtime. Evaluated across two agent frameworks and five LLM backbones, DyMalSkill motivates a system-level defense that achieved 0% observed attack success across all 12 tested malicious behaviors while preserving all 300 benign skill task completions.

[paper]
* Equal contribution

Divide and Doubt: Diverse Distributed Poisoning for RAG

Tianhao Chen et al.

First-author manuscript, 2026 · Under review, ACL Rolling Review (August 2026 cycle)

Investigates the robustness of retrieval-augmented generation under diverse, distributed corpus poisoning, including clustering-based defenses. Across 27 model–configuration settings, mean attack success increases from 53.4% to 67.4% in black-box evaluation and from 52.3% to 68.4% in white-box evaluation.

Manuscript

ToolDetect: Code-Based Malicious Agent-Tool Detection

Tianhao Chen et al.

Co-first-author manuscript · Under review, ICLR 2027

Screens Python agent tools before installation using AST-verified behavior anchors and two-pass, context-aware committee adjudication. Evaluated against 12 scanners and detectors, it reaches 91.8% true positive rate at 0.5% false positive rate on a 2,400-instance tool benchmark, and 94.0% at 0.25% on a separate 800-instance skill benchmark.

Manuscript

Offline RL for Sepsis Treatment with Synthetic EHR Data

Tianhao Chen et al.

Contributing author · Manuscript in progress · Advised by Prof. Pan Xu

Investigates when synthetic electronic health records improve offline reinforcement learning for sepsis treatment. Adapts TimeDiff, HealthGen, and MTGAN to MIMIC-IV trajectories, with likelihood-based data filtering. Ongoing evaluation compares real/synthetic data mixtures and asks which data-quality metrics predict downstream policy stability.

Ongoing Research

Resource-to-Skill Poisoning in Retrieval-Augmented Skill Creation

Studies whether untrusted retrieved documents can contaminate reusable agent skills, creating risks that persist beyond the original task and session. Preliminary experiments examine persistence, attack success, and benign task utility as separate outcomes; evaluation is ongoing.
